What should an agency automate first?

Not everything. Start with the one task that happens every week, follows the same steps, and costs you clients when it slips.

Start with a simple test

Before you automate anything, ask three questions about the task:

  • Does it happen every week? If it only happens once a quarter, the setup effort won't pay off.
  • Does it follow the same steps? Tasks that need fresh judgement every time are hard to automate well.
  • Does it hurt when it's late or forgotten? If nothing bad happens, it isn't a priority.

A task that gets three yeses is a good place to start.

For most agencies, the answer is the same

It's what happens after a new enquiry arrives. Enquiries come in every week. The steps are nearly identical each time. And a slow or forgotten reply can quietly send a good client to another agency.

Compare that with something like monthly invoicing. It matters, but it's usually already handled, and a day's delay rarely loses you a client.

What to automate in that flow

  • Saving the enquiry. Every message from your form or inbox goes into your CRM or tracker automatically.
  • Pulling out the details. Service, timing and budget are picked out, and anything missing is flagged.
  • Drafting the reply. A first reply is written for the right person to review and send.
  • Reminding the owner. If nobody follows up by a set time, someone gets a nudge.
  • Setting up new clients. When a deal is won, the project folder and checklist are created.

Kernel's tip: Don't automate sending. Let AI draft, and let a person on your team press send. You get the speed without the risk.

What not to automate first

  • Sending replies with no human check. One wrong message can cost more than it saves.
  • Creative work. That's what clients pay you for. Use AI to help your team, not replace their judgement.
  • Rare tasks. Things that happen a few times a year are cheaper to keep doing by hand.

How to start small

  1. Pick one place enquiries come from, like your website form.
  2. Walk through one recent enquiry and write down every step that happened.
  3. Note how long the first reply took, so you have something to compare against.
  4. Automate the saving, sorting and reminders first. Keep replies human-approved.
  5. Run it for a few weeks, then decide what to add next.

The short version

Automate the boring, repeatable steps around new enquiries first. Keep people in charge of what clients see. Start with one source, measure the before and after, and grow from there.
